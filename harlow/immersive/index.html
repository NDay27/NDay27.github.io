Day 1

Stakes in the ground

We walk the lot with you, then stake the corners so you can stand where the kitchen sink will be.

Weeks 1 to 3

Footings and foundation

Dig, pour footings, set the walls. Nothing goes up until the base is square to within a quarter inch.

  • Soil and footing inspection, passed
  • Foundation walls cured 7 days
Weeks 4 to 6

Floor deck and first walls

Joists, subfloor, then walls at 16 inches on center with real headers over every opening. You can walk your rooms by week six.

Weeks 7 to 9

Second floor and rafters

Upstairs walls, then rafters to the ridge. This is the week most families come out for pictures.

Weeks 10 to 12

Dried in

Sheathing, house wrap, roof deck and windows. Once it's dried in, the weather stops being a schedule problem.

  • Framing inspection, passed
  • Windows set and flashed
Weeks 13 to 18

Siding, roof and porch

Board-and-batten, standing-seam metal and the timber porch outside, while the inside crews run wiring, plumbing and drywall.

Weeks 19 to 34

Finishes, then the keys

Cabinets, floors, paint, sod and the final walkthrough. We fix the punch list before you sign off, not after.

Move-in night

Porch lights on.

Same plan, same crew, start to finish. And a 2-year workmanship warranty from the day you move in.

    Questions people ask first

    Do you build on my lot, or do I need to buy one of yours?

    Either. Most of our homes go on land the family already owns. If you're still looking, we'll walk lots with you before you buy and tell you what the site will cost to build on.

    Can I bring my own plans?

    Yes. We price your architect's plans as drawn, or our designer can draw a plan with you. Either way you get one fixed price before anything starts.

    What happens if I want to change something mid-build?

    You get a written change order with the price and any schedule impact. Nothing changes until you sign it, so the final bill never surprises you.

    How do payments work?

    10% at signing, then draws tied to finished stages: foundation, framing, dried in, drywall and final. Most families pay through their construction loan.

    How far do you travel?

    About 30 miles from Morton: Peoria, East Peoria, Washington, Pekin, Tremont, Eureka, Germantown Hills, Metamora and the towns in between.
